# Apex Process Solutions — Risk Register Audit and Retention Policy

**Document type:** Fictional company policy (continuity with Modules 4–7)
**Source:** Self-created fictional example for classroom use — no real company, client, or
confidential information

## Purpose

This policy defines how long RAID log and escalation-note records must be retained after a
project closes, and what an internal audit checks when reviewing a project's risk
governance.

## Retention rule

The full RAID log, all escalation notes, and the monthly budget variance logs for a
project must be retained for **24 months after project closure**, stored in the PMO's
shared archive, not on an individual PM's local drive. Entries must not be deleted or
edited after closure — corrections are added as new dated entries, not overwrites.

## What an internal audit checks

When the PMO conducts a quarterly audit of a closed project, it checks:

- That every risk rated Certain/High probability and High impact was actually escalated,
  per the PMO Risk Management Policy's escalation rule (not just logged).
- That every escalation note followed the five-part structure from the Escalation &
  Steering Committee Communication Guide.
- That every budget variance over 10% has a matching escalation record, per the Budget
  Variance Escalation Policy.
- That RAID log entries do not contain data the Data Handling section of the PMO Risk
  Management Policy prohibits (client account numbers, individual client employee names).

## Example

*"A quarterly audit finds a risk logged as Certain probability and High impact, but no
escalation note exists for it."* This is a policy violation under this audit policy,
because the escalation rule in the PMO Risk Management Policy required escalation once
that probability/impact combination was reached, regardless of whether the risk later
resolved on its own.

## Why this matters for AI-assisted PMO tools

An audit question requires cross-referencing this retention/audit policy against the
underlying escalation rule in a different document — a model must retrieve the specific
rule being checked, not just this policy's retention duration, to answer correctly.

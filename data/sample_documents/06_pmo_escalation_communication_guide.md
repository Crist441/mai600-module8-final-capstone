# Apex Process Solutions — PMO Escalation & Steering Committee Communication Guide

**Document type:** Fictional company policy (continuity with Module 4/5 prompt sets)
**Source:** Self-created fictional example for classroom use — no real company, client, or
confidential information

## Purpose

This guide defines how a risk or issue should be written up before it reaches the
steering committee, so that non-technical stakeholders can act on it quickly.

## Required structure for an escalation note

Every escalation note must include, in this order:

1. **Headline** — one sentence stating the risk/issue and why it needs attention now.
2. **Current status** — what is known as of today, stated as fact, not speculation.
3. **What is not yet known** — explicitly separate from "current status," so the
   committee does not treat an assumption as a confirmed fact.
4. **Recommended next action** — a specific, assignable next step, not a vague
   "continue monitoring."
5. **Decision needed from the committee, if any** — state clearly if no decision is
   needed and the note is for awareness only.

## Tone requirements

Escalation notes must be written for a non-technical executive audience: no
internal jargon (e.g., avoid terms like "race condition," "staging table," or
"retry logic" without a plain-language explanation), no blame language directed at
a specific team or client, and no invented numbers or dates that were not confirmed
by the project team.

## Why this matters for AI-assisted PMO tools

This is the structure and tone standard an AI-generated executive summary or
escalation note should be checked against — it is company-specific formatting
guidance that a general-purpose model would not know without retrieval.
